A playful corner of GitHub

Psanctuary is an inter-tradition church built around a sincere sacred mushroom practice. Its real home is psanctuary.org, where Sunday service, membership, courses, and events all live. This GitHub organization is its decentralized, playful side: a place to try tools the community can own, with a little more color, and every change written down in the open.

The church holds two GitHub organizations. Its sibling, psanctuarychurch, is the church's professional face on GitHub. You're in psanctuary, where the experiments live: one day, perhaps, a small network of the community's own and a DAO to go with it (there's a sneak peek).

How a decentralized church can work

A mushroom has no headquarters. Underground, its mycelium links roots across a whole forest and moves water and nutrients to wherever they're needed. This side of Psanctuary asks what a church could look like if it grew the same way. These are ideas being explored in the open, not how the church is run today.

  • Many hands, no single center

    Members gather in the church's member network, where member-led clubs meet. Alana tends the Maine frontier and Christopher W the Pennsylvania one. Like hyphae, each reaches where it's needed.

  • Deciding together Sneak peek

    A network of the community's own, on open protocols like Lens and Farcaster, paired with a Psanctuary DAO where members propose ideas and decide together. Privacy first.

    The network and DAO card

  • Tokens of gratitude Sneak peek

    Soulbound, non-transferable tokens of gratitude, from Christopher W's gratitude-token project: a thank-you that stays in the wallet it's given to. It can't be sold, sent on or traded, and it isn't money. Still being tested.

    A first look, on the pledge page

  • A pledge, signed, not sent

    Here already: sign a short pledge of support with a crypto wallet. It's free and it sends nothing. Nothing is recorded, and there are no counts.
